# Privacy Concepts

> **Summary:** The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry shows no single agreed definition of privacy; it surveys competing accounts. Entry first published 2002-05-14, substantive revision 2023-10-19; author not captured. Read 100,000 of 160,904 characters.

## What it is

Competing accounts listed in the entry (names and years as recorded in the source note):
- the right to be left alone (Warren and Brandeis, 1890);
- control over information (Westin 1967, Fried 1968);
- inaccessibility (Gavison 1980; quoted: "An individual enjoys perfect privacy when he is completely inaccessible to others.");
- a cluster concept (DeCew 1997);
- contextual integrity (Nissenbaum 2010);
- Solove (2008) calls it a "concept in disarray".

> **Informational privacy**: norms letting people control who knows what about them; the German "informational self-determination" appears in 1983.

## How it works

Debate is conceptual, not technical. Link to engineering: OWASP's threat modeling page lists LINDDUN as a privacy threat technique (see [[Threat Modeling and Risk]]); the law is in [[GDPR Data Protection Principles]].
