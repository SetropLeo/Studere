# Proof by Contradiction

> **Summary:** To prove a claim, assume its negation and derive a contradiction; the assumption was then false, so the claim holds. One read source, so confidence is `uncertain`.

## What it is

> **Proof by contradiction** (reductio ad absurdum): assume the negation of the claim; if this leads to a contradiction, "the original assumption of ¬X must have been false" ([[Davies - A Cool Brisk Walk - Proof]], 9.2).

## How it works

Example from the source: the square root of 2 is irrational. Assume it equals a/b in lowest terms; both a and b end up even, contradicting "lowest terms" ([[Davies - A Cool Brisk Walk - Proof]], 9.2).

## Key terms

> **Negation (¬X)**: the statement that X is false; see [[Propositional Logic]].
> **Contradiction**: deriving both a statement and its negation (archivist gloss, not Davies's wording).

## Example

See above (√2).
