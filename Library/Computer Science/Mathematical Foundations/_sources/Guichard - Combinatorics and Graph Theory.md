# Guichard - Combinatorics and Graph Theory (online book)

## Why it is reliable (or not)

Open college textbook by a Whitman College mathematics professor; HTML version from TeX source (D. Farmer). Textbook = level A. Independent of Levin. Slightly more advanced (generating-function approach). No edition/date stated; the title is paraphrased from the site's table of contents/URL (cgt), not copied from a title page. Pages read: book index, sections 1.2, 1.6, 3.4.

## Extracted points

- 1.2: P(n,k) = n!/(n-k)!; a permutation is "a particular linear ordering of the objects"; 0! = 1; C(n,k) = P(n,k)/k! = n!/(k!(n-k)!), each k-subset counted k! times in P(n,k).
- 1.6 Pigeonhole Principle (Thm 1.6.1); Cor 1.6.7: n(r-1)+1 objects in n boxes gives a box with at least r objects; Thm 1.6.6 is the per-box weighted generalization.
- 3.4 Recurrence relations solved by generating functions: f(x) = sum a_i x^i; Fibonacci gives f(x) = x/(1-x-x^2) and the closed form F_i = (1/sqrt5)((1+sqrt5)/2)^i - (1/sqrt5)((1-sqrt5)/2)^i.

## Relevant excerpts

> "Suppose that n+1 (or more) objects are put into n boxes. Then some box contains at least two objects." (Thm 1.6.1)

> "Among any 13 people, at least two share a birth month." (Example 1.6.2)
