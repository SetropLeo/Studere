## Extracted points

- Partial order on a set X: a relation that is reflexive, antisymmetric, transitive; a set with one is a poset (partially ordered set).
- Lattice: a poset in which every pair of elements has a least upper bound (join, a v b) and a greatest lower bound (meet, a ^ b).
- Examples: integers/rationals/reals under <= (posets); power set P(X) under inclusion is a lattice (join = union, meet = intersection); subgroups of a group under inclusion (meet = intersection, join = subgroup generated by the union); divisors of 24 under divisibility.
- Lattice operations are commutative, associative, idempotent and satisfy absorption laws; conversely, a set with two operations satisfying these laws is a lattice (order view and algebra view are equivalent).
- Chapter intro: lattices generalize order relations; Boolean algebras generalize intersection and union; applications in logic, circuit theory, probability.
