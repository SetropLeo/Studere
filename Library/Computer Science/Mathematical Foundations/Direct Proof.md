# Direct Proof

> **Summary:** A direct proof starts from what is known and moves by positive steps to the conclusion. Content is supported by one read source, so confidence is `uncertain`.

## What it is

> **Proof**: "a chain of reasoning" in which each step can be logically deduced from the ones that preceded it, starting from axioms, "the things we're allowed to start with"; the result is a **theorem** ([[Davies - A Cool Brisk Walk - Proof]], 9.1; the phrases in quotes are Davies's wording, the rest is paraphrase).
> **Direct proof**: "start from what's known and proceed directly by positive steps towards your conclusion" ([[Davies - A Cool Brisk Walk - Proof]], 9.2).

## How it works

Chain known facts to the claim. Alternatives are [[Proof by Contradiction]] and [[Mathematical Induction]]. [[Hammack - Book of Proof]] has a chapter on direct proof (Ch. 4) and one on contrapositive proof (Ch. 5), confirmed by the Third Edition table of contents (open.umn.edu); the chapter text was not read, so it is kept as a pointer and no content is taken from it.

## Key terms

> **Axiom**: a starting statement accepted without proof.
> **Theorem**: a statement established by proof.
