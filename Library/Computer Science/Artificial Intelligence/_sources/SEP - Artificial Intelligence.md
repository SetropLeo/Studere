# SEP - Artificial Intelligence

## Why it is reliable (or not)

Refereed, curated encyclopedia entry maintained by Stanford; long (about 198k characters) and covers history, approaches, logic, probability and philosophy. Fetch read the first 100k characters in detail and a summary of the rest. The entry is philosophically oriented and follows the Russell-Norvig agent view; not a how-to for engineers.

## Extracted points

- Section 1 "The History of AI": Dartmouth conference 1956, Turing 1950, precursors such as Descartes and logic.
- Section 3 "Approaches to AI": 3.1 intelligent agent continuum; 3.2 logic-based (symbolic/logicist) AI; 3.3 non-logicist AI (symbolic non-logicist and connectionist/neural); 3.4 beyond the clash of paradigms.
- Later parts cover probability (Kolmogorov axioms, joint/conditional probability, Bayesian networks), probabilistic machine learning and PAC analysis, applications, machine ethics, strong vs weak AI (Chinese Room, Godelian and Dreyfus arguments), and future/Singularity debates.

## Relevant excerpts

> "We define AI as the study of agents that receive percepts from the environment and perform actions." (intelligent agent section; attributed to Russell and Norvig)

> "Philosophical AI is AI, not philosophy; but it's AI rooted in and flowing from, philosophy." (philosophical AI section)
