# What Artificial Intelligence Is - History and Approaches

> **Summary:** The SEP entry follows Russell and Norvig in defining AI as the study of agents that receive percepts and perform actions, and organizes the field into logic-based, non-logicist (including connectionist) and agent-centered approaches. Single-source note (one encyclopedia entry read via a fetch summary).

## What it is

- Definition (SEP, attributed there to Russell and Norvig): AI is the study of agents that receive percepts from the environment and perform actions. The quotation is as recorded in the source note; recheck against the page.
- SEP's table of contents for the history section names the Dartmouth conference of 1956 and Turing in 1950, plus precursors such as Descartes and logic. The source note records only these names, not what each contributed. Details of the history are not in the library (see gaps).
- SEP section 3 "Approaches to AI" is organized as: the intelligent agent continuum; logic-based (symbolic, logicist) AI; non-logicist AI (symbolic non-logicist and connectionist/neural); and "beyond the clash of paradigms".
- The same entry has later parts on probability, probabilistic machine learning, machine ethics and strong versus weak AI (Chinese Room, Godelian and Dreyfus arguments) as recorded in the source note. The library does not record the content of those arguments.

## Key terms

> **Strong vs weak AI**: SEP's later parts discuss this distinction through the Chinese Room and related arguments; the definitions themselves were not captured in the source note (gloss, unsourced).

> **Connectionist AI**: the neural-network branch of non-logicist AI, per SEP's section list.
